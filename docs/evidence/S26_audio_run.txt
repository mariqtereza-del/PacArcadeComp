S26 evidence: live audio (src/audio/audio_out.{h,cpp}, src/app/main.cpp)
Date: 2026-10-09

Design: sound chip (96 kHz, 1,584 samples per frame, registers and the 5001 sound-enable latch applied once per frame) -> exact area-averaging resampler to the card's rate (48 kHz asked; SDL converts if the card refuses) -> lock-free ring buffer (32,768 samples) -> SDL audio callback (480-sample = 10 ms device buffer). A controller nudges the resampler step by at most +/-2.5% from a smoothed ring level (target 12 ms just before each new frame) so the long-term rate follows the card's real clock; underruns are counted in the callback, the delay is measured there.

== What else was checked ==
- Resampler alone (scratch check, not stored): a 440 Hz tone 96k -> 48k keeps its pitch (439.75 Hz by crossing count, 0.06% resolution), peak level 11998 of 12000; a 30 kHz tone (above the new limit) is cut from 12000 to 6539; 1% scale changes give 1% pitch changes as designed.
- Data path: the 4.2 s jingle captured from the sound-card callback has exactly the same peak amplitude as the offline render (19796) and the same notes.
- Finding: SDL's test (dummy) audio driver runs its clock about 1.7% slow and, with a 512-sample buffer, 6.7% FAST (it sleeps whole milliseconds). With 512 the controller (then limited to 0.5%) could not follow and underran 15% of the time; the limit was raised to 2.5%, the level smoothed (0.8 s) and the buffer set to 480 samples (exactly 10 ms). In the final runs the notes are about 1.7% sharp because the controller follows that slow test clock: this is the design working, and a real card is within a few hundredths of a percent.
- Regression: picture dumps at frames 700 and 1500 still equal the S22 dumps (0 differing pixels); --autoplay still passes, also the Windows exe under wine.
